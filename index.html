How it works

Two things are always in play

The counterpart in every scenario tracks how good the deal is for you and how much they trust you. Most moves trade one against the other.

They react to what you say

Each reply depends on the move you chose. Push too hard and trust drops. Let it fall far enough and they end the conversation with no deal.

You get a debrief

At the end you see your result, the tactic behind every move, and a note on what it earned or cost you. Then run it again and try a different path.

What the scenarios teach

Anchor with evidence
Open with a specific number you can justify. The first credible number tends to shape where the conversation lands.
Ask about their constraints
A no on budget or staffing usually hides options. Asking what is actually possible turns a refusal into a set of choices.
Trade instead of conceding
When you give something, ask for something back. Free concessions teach the other side that pushing works.
Widen the pie
Bring in more than one issue, such as timing, title, or a bonus, so both sides have more ways to say yes.
Name the impact without blaming
Describe what happened and what it cost you. Facts and effects are much harder to argue with than accusations.
Lock it in
End with a specific agreement, a check-in date, or a note in writing, so the deal survives a busy month.
